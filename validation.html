How closely does this app reproduce the paper?

Faigenbaum-Golovin et al., "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: unveiling text authorship", PLOS ONE 2025. This page is generated by tools/build-validation.js using the same engine and data the app uses.

What we did

Data. Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible (the same source as the paper), Genesis–Kings. Each word is reduced to lemma tokens: a written word split into its lemma pieces, proper names collapsed to one <Np> token and gentilics to <Ng>, and pronominal suffixes kept as their own tokens. These rules were tuned until our token counts matched the authors' published spreadsheet for all 50 tested chapters exactly (34,294 tokens in total). Qere variant words are counted, as the authors do.

Corpora. The three ground-truth corpora (D, DtrH, P) use the chapter lists of the paper's Table S1. The preprint's text and its Table 1 disagree about which P chapters are included (Gen 1 versus Lev 4); we followed the table. The P corpus also includes the auxiliary narrative verses, as counts only (never tested).

Statistics. For each word in a pair of texts, an exact two-sided binomial test gives a p-value. Higher Criticism (γ₀ = 0.35) summarises these over the words present in the pair. Each corpus chapter is scored the same way against the rest of its corpus (leave-one-out), and the test chapter's score is compared with those by a t-test. The attribution is the corpus with the largest p-value. Each tested chapter is left out of its own corpus.

Tried without improvement. Several variations of the token rules (compound lemmas, suffixes, proper names) either moved token counts away from the authors' spreadsheet or did not bring the p-values closer.

Known differences

  • Tokens for the 50 chapters match; the auxiliary text does not (above).
  • Dictionary sizes are within a few words of the paper's but not equal.
  • The remaining p-value differences are unexplained. Per-word p-values we spot-checked against the authors' discriminating-word lists agreed for a minority of words (roughly 20–40% within about 12%, depending on the corpus).
  • The paper's 2 Kgs 17 is verses 1–21 only, as in the app.
